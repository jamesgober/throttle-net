# throttle-net v2.1.0

Fixes from an independent review of 2.0.0. No call site changes; two methods
are added. Requires better-bucket 1.2.

## A pause no longer ends in a burst

When a downstream answers 429 with a `Retry-After`, the throttle pauses. Its
reservations kept maturing on their original schedule during the pause, so
when it ended, every waiter they covered went in the same instant, together
with the tokens that had refilled: on a 10/s throttle with a burst of 10, a
5 s pause released 50 requests within 10 ms. That is the burst a provider that
just asked for a pause is least likely to accept.

Now a pause longer than the time the bucket takes to fill from empty moves the
schedule later by the difference, and every waiter moves its own due time with
it. When the pause ends, at most one burst goes, waiters included, and the
rest follow at the configured rate, in the order they reserved. A pause no
longer than the fill time changes nothing, and an idle or drained throttle is
still full after a long pause, as before.

`Throttle::postponed` and `Limiter::postponed` report the total time pauses
have moved a limiter's schedule, for callers that hold a reservation from
`reserve` themselves. The waiting `acquire` methods use it already.

## Composites and sliding logs

A composite (`Hybrid`, `Layered`, `MultiLimiter`, the tower layer) that took a
`SlidingWindowLog`'s unit and then slept out a pause on another constituent
sent its request after the unit had left the window, so the request went
uncounted, and cancelling it refunded a newer grant. Composites, and waiters
on a sliding log, now give back what they hold while paused and take it again
when the request can go. `SlidingWindowLog::refund` now documents that a
refund is exact only while the refunded grant is still in the window.

## Adaptive limits

Failures of one congestion event that settled at the same moment on different
threads could each halve the limit, which is the collapse the epoch check was
meant to stop. A decrease now claims the epoch with one compare-exchange, so
one event decreases the limit once, however the failures interleave.

## Settling usage

`MultiLimiter::settle_costs` charged any reported overspend as debt with no
ceiling. One bogus usage count of `u32::MAX` on a 30,000 tokens per minute
dimension made every later call wait about 99 days. The overspend charged for
one call is now capped at the dimension's capacity, one refill.
`settle_costs_uncapped` keeps the old behaviour for counts you trust.
`Throttle::charge` and the per-key store's debt restore now take one step
whatever the debt, through better-bucket's `charge_at`.

## Compatibility

MSRV 1.85, as before. `Limiter::postponed` is a provided method, so existing
implementations compile unchanged.
